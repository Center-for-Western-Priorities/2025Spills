Colorado Spills Report 2025

Source: Energy and Carbon Management Commission (ECMC)  |  Recent spills, January 1 – December 31, 2025

Colorado averaged 467,000 barrels of crude oil per day in 2025, essentially flat from 465,000 in 2024. Production is concentrated in the Denver-Julesburg Basin, with Weld County in northeastern Colorado accounting for the vast majority of the state's output and the overwhelming share of its spill record.

The Energy and Carbon Management Commission recorded 338 spills in 2025, the lowest annual count in the data series since 2016 and down from 378 in 2024. Despite the decline in incidents, total volume spilled nearly doubled, rising from 436,000 gallons in 2024 to 794,000 gallons in 2025. A small number of large events drove that increase. Weld County accounted for the majority of incidents, as it has in every year on record.

The gap between a falling spill count and a rising spill volume points to a pattern seen across the industry: routine small spills have declined, but when significant failures occur they tend to release more material. Produced water was the dominant substance by volume, consistent with the Wattenberg field's high water-to-oil production ratios.

Policy and Regulation

Rules and regulations

Regulatory oversight. Colorado's oil and gas spill program is administered by the Energy and Carbon Management Commission (ECMC), the state agency formerly known as the Colorado Oil and Gas Conservation Commission (COGCC). The agency was renamed in 2023, reflecting a statutory mandate under Senate Bill 19-181 to prioritize public health, safety, welfare, and the environment alongside resource development.

What must be reported. ECMC Rule 906 requires operators to report any spill or release of E&P waste or produced fluids that meets at least one of the following thresholds: any release that threatens waters of the state, a public water system, a residence or occupied structure, livestock, wildlife, or a public road; any release of one barrel or more outside of secondary containment; or any release of five barrels or more regardless of containment. The five-barrel threshold means that minor, fully contained spills still require formal reporting.

Reporting timeline. Operators must submit an initial notification within 24 hours of discovery, verbally, by email, or via Form 19. If the initial notification was not filed on Form 19, a Form 19 Initial must follow within 72 hours. A Form 19 Supplemental is required within 10 days of discovery.

Recent regulatory changes. In October 2024, ECMC adopted the Cumulative Impacts and Enhanced Systems and Practices Rules, which took effect December 15, 2024. Those rules expand requirements for operators to assess and mitigate cumulative environmental impacts near communities but do not materially change the spill reporting thresholds described above.

Enforcement. ECMC's Compliance Specialists conduct on-site inspections of oil and gas locations statewide; every well in Colorado is on an inspection schedule. When non-compliance is identified, ECMC may pursue voluntary correction through outreach or warning letters, or escalate to a formal Notice of Alleged Violation (NOAV). More serious violations are referred to the ECMC Enforcement Unit, which calculates penalties that are then adjudicated by the Commission. SB 19-181 strengthened the Commission's enforcement authority and directed it to prioritize public health and environmental outcomes in penalty determinations.

Methodology

Data and methodology

Data source: Energy and Carbon Management Commission (ECMC), formerly COGCC, renamed in 2023. Source file: ColoradoSpills.xlsm, downloaded from the ECMC online spill database.

Record selection: Filtered to rows where Spill Type = "Recent" and Date of Discovery falls between January 1 and December 31, 2025. Each unique Tracking # is counted as one spill event; where multiple rows share a Tracking #, the first occurrence is used for categorical fields (operator, county, facility type, surface owner). Spill count: 338 unique spills.

Trend chart: Covers 2016–2025. The 2014 data point (2 spills) was excluded as an isolated outlier predating the continuous reporting series. Total since 2016: 3,890.

Volume: ECMC records volume across six liquid columns (Oil, Condensate, Produced Water, Drilling Fluid, Flow Back Fluid, and Other E&P Waste BBLs Spilled). For each Tracking #, the maximum reported value per column is taken across all associated rows (to avoid double-counting supplemental filings), then all six column maxima are summed and multiplied by 42 to convert barrels to gallons. Volume figures are minimums based on operator self-reporting; a substantial share of spills list unknown volume.

Distance to sensitive resources: ECMC records the distance in feet from the spill site to the nearest surface water body, water well, and occupied building. For each spill, the minimum reported distance across all rows is used. Only spills reporting a non-zero distance of 5,280 feet (one mile) or less are included. Results are bucketed into six 500-foot ranges.

Inorganic-only filings: This analysis includes all Form 19 filings meeting the date and spill-type criteria. It does not filter out filings where detected contamination consists solely of inorganic parameters — such as pH, sodium adsorption ratio (SAR), electrical conductivity, or arsenic — that may reflect naturally occurring soil chemistry rather than a hydrocarbon release. ECMC does not provide a formal flag for these cases; the determination appears, when made, only in free-text supplemental report fields. These filings represent less than 0.5% of the 2025 recent record and have no material effect on any figure in this report.
