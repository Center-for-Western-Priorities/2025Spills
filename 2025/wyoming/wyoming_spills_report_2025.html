Wyoming Spills Report 2025

Source: Wyoming Oil and Gas Conservation Commission (WOGCC)  |  Reported January 1 – December 31, 2025

Wyoming averaged roughly 289,000 barrels of crude oil per day in 2025, down slightly from 292,000 in 2024 and a fraction of New Mexico's output but enough to make the state one of the top ten oil producers in the country. The Powder River Basin in the northeast, anchored by Converse and Campbell counties, drives the bulk of Wyoming's production and accounts for the majority of its spills.

The Wyoming Oil and Gas Conservation Commission recorded 786 spill incidents in 2025, down 39 from 825 in 2024. Total volume fell 21% from 1.81 million gallons in 2024 to 1.43 million gallons in 2025, with produced water accounting for nearly all of the decline—dropping from 1.53 million gallons to 1.19 million. Oil volume held essentially flat at about 191,000 gallons in both years. Converse County led the state with 172 spills, followed by Campbell at 137. Together, the two Powder River Basin counties accounted for 39% of all incidents statewide.

The volume figures were shaped significantly by a handful of large events. Merit Energy reported the highest operator volume at 304,000 gallons despite ranking only seventh in spill count, reflecting a pattern of low-frequency, high-volume incidents. Crowheart Energy led all operators in count with 102 spills but ranked fifth in volume at 62,000 gallons.

Historical Trends

Spills Per Year, 2018–2025

Wyoming recorded 786 spills in 2025, continuing a pattern of year-to-year variation with no clear long-term trend. Incident counts peaked in 2019 and 2024 (both 825) and fell to a low of 487 in 2022. Note: the 2018 figure (722) reflects incidents with a report date in 2018 only; the source file also contains records from 2016 and 2017.

Total Volume Spilled Per Year, 2018–2025 (Gallons)

Total spill volume has varied significantly year to year, with 2018 the highest on record at 3.49 million gallons and 2022 the lowest at 1.15 million. The 2018 figure is influenced by several large produced-water incidents. Volume in 2025 (1.43 million gallons) was the second-lowest in the series.

Geography

Spills by County: 2024 vs. 2025

Converse County led Wyoming with 172 spills, slightly down from 174 in 2024. Campbell County ranked second. Both counties sit atop the Powder River Basin, Wyoming's most active oil region. Laramie County fell sharply from 69 to 33, while Carbon rose from 73 to 83.

Year-Over-Year

Total Volume Spilled: 2024 vs. 2025 (Gallons)

Total spill volume fell 21 percent from 2024 to 2025, dropping from 1.81 million gallons to 1.43 million gallons. The decline was driven almost entirely by a reduction in produced water discharge, which fell from 1.53 million gallons in 2024 to 1.19 million in 2025—a drop of roughly 340,000 gallons. Oil volume held essentially flat at about 191,000 gallons in both years. The "other" category, which includes drilling fluid and other materials, fell by about 40,000 gallons. Incident count also declined, from 825 in 2024 to 786 in 2025.

Policy and Regulation

Rules and regulations

Regulatory oversight. Wyoming's oil and gas spill program is administered by the Wyoming Oil and Gas Conservation Commission (WOGCC), a state agency under the Wyoming Department of Audit. Spill reporting requirements are established under Chapter 4, Section 4-3 of the WOGCC Administrative Rules.

What must be reported. Wyoming uses a tiered reporting system based on spill volume. Operators must maintain internal records of all spills, including those under one barrel. Spills between one and ten barrels require a written report within 15 working days but no verbal notification to the Commission. Spills of ten barrels or more require verbal notification to the WOGCC by the next business day, followed by a written Form 17 Spill or Release Report within 15 working days. Wyoming does not apply a separate trigger based on proximity to sensitive resources, nor does it require reporting of gas venting or flaring through this system.

Enforcement. The WOGCC enforces compliance through field inspections, audits, and issuance of Notices of Alleged Violation (NOAVs). Operators found in violation must take corrective action within a Commission-specified timeframe; failure to comply can result in financial penalties or operational restrictions. Wyoming's spill counts in this report are substantially lower than New Mexico's, in part because Wyoming's tiered thresholds exclude the smallest releases from mandatory Commission reporting.

Methodology

Data and methodology

Data source: Wyoming Oil and Gas Conservation Commission (WOGCC). Source files: WyomingSpills2025.xlsx (2025 spills) and WyomingSpills2024.xlsx (2024 spills, used for year-over-year comparisons).

Record selection: All spill records in the source files are included; no spill-type filter is applied (WOGCC data does not use the Recent/Historical distinction used by ECMC). Each row represents one reported spill event.

Volume: Volume units vary by row. Rows where Unit = "Bbls" are converted to gallons by multiplying by 42; rows where Unit = "Gal" are used as reported. Other unit values are excluded from volume totals.

Operator names: Operator names in the WOGCC source data include legal suffixes and spelling variations. Names are standardized by stripping common suffixes (LLC, LP, INC, ENERGY, RESOURCES, OPERATING, CO, COMPANY, and variants) and applying manual corrections for known cases including CITATION, CONTANGO, and PEAK POWDER RIVER.

County maps: 2024 and 2025 county-level spill counts are displayed as side-by-side choropleth maps using a shared color scale, so shading is directly comparable across years. The shared maximum is the highest county count across both years combined.
